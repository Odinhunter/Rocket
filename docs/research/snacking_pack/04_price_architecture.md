# Lane 4 — Price architecture

**Scope:** real MRPs and pack sizes on the Indian urban snacking shelf, the ₹5→₹100+ ladder, magic price points, shrinkflation, and the trade-up multiple from mainstream to better-for-you.

**All listing observations dated 2026-08-14** unless otherwise stated. Every price below comes from a page actually opened, or is explicitly marked as unverified and quarantined in OPEN QUESTIONS.

---

## Key findings

1. **The Indian snacking shelf is priced in coins, not in rupees-per-gram.** The manufacturer picks the price point first (₹5, ₹10, ₹20) and then solves for grammage. This is stated outright by management: an analyst on Britannia's Q3 FY25 call framed it as "in the popular price point of, say, INR5 and INR10, till now how much grammage cut you would have taken **because there you can't increase prices**" — and Britannia did not dispute the premise.

2. **₹10 is the pivot of the whole shelf.** Parle-G's 90 g pack carries a printed MRP of exactly ₹10 (verified). Britannia has said its ₹5 and ₹10 low-unit packs are ~50–55% of total mix. Everything below ₹10 is defended by shrinking the pack; everything above ₹10 is where price actually moves. Britannia's own stated policy, as reported in May 2026: "selective price hikes through a mix of grammage reduction in smaller packs and price increases in packs priced above ₹10."

3. **Chocolate's entry point has drifted from ₹5 to ₹10, and the new-launch entry point is now ₹20.** Mondelez's September 2025 launch, Cadbury Dairy Milk Milkinis, entered at **₹20 for 17 g** and ₹40 for a 34 g twin-pack — i.e. a new Gen-Z-targeted format launched at double the historic impulse coin.

4. **The trade-up gap is roughly 2.5–2.9× per gram and 4.7–6.1× per unit.** A RiteBite Max Protein bar is ₹1.78/g against Cadbury Dairy Milk's ₹0.71/g. In cash-out-of-pocket terms — which is what an impulse buyer actually feels — a ₹17 Dairy Milk becomes an ₹80 protein bar. **A "healthy alternative to a chocolate bar" is asking for roughly five times the cash at the counter.** See the dedicated section below.

5. **Modern e-commerce is not the impulse shelf, and must not be used to model impulse behaviour.** Flipkart searches for "Dairy Milk 13.2 g" return gift hampers and 5×/8× multipacks; Flipkart's Cadbury category page has essentially no single-unit impulse SKU on it. The ₹10/₹20 single unit lives in kirana and quick commerce. This is the single biggest channel divergence in the lane.

6. **The street comparator has moved under the snack shelf.** A single pav went from ~₹3 to ~₹4 in Mumbai by May 2026, pushing vada pav upward. The ₹10 Parle-G pack and the ₹10–₹15 cutting chai remain the reference points an ordinary buyer prices a snack against.

## The price ladder (₹5 / ₹10 / ₹20 / ₹30 / ₹50 / ₹100+)

**₹5 — the defended floor, biscuits and namkeen only.**
There is no mainstream ₹5 chocolate bar left. ₹5 is a biscuit and small-namkeen point, held by grammage rather than price. Parle cut its ₹5 pack from 70 g to 65 g as far back as Dec 2018 and has kept cutting since. Britannia has said ₹5 and ₹10 LUPs together are ~50–55% of its mix. Margin at this rung is near zero by design — it exists for penetration and for the daily chai-dip occasion, not profit.

**₹10 — the true centre of gravity.**
- Parle-G 90 g, MRP ₹10 (verified). This is the single most important price-grammage pair on the Indian shelf.
- Chocolate's impulse entry sits here (Dairy Milk 13.2 g is the widely-cited ₹10 unit, though I could not open a listing to verify it — see OPEN QUESTIONS).
- A cutting chai in a metro also sits at roughly ₹10, which is why ₹10 reads to a buyer as "one small thing", not as a considered purchase.

**₹20 — the new-launch and bridge-pack rung.**
- Cadbury Dairy Milk Milkinis launched here at 17 g (Sept 2025) — notable, because a *new* chocolate format entering at ₹20 rather than ₹10 signals the coin point has stepped up.
- Britannia explicitly built a bridge pack down to this rung in an adjacent category: "we talked about drinks where we are now coming up with a INR20 SKU, which is a very large market and our participation was only in the INR35 or INR40" (Varun Berry, Q3 FY25 call). That is textbook bridge-pack logic — reach down to a coin point you don't currently participate in.
- Lay's and Kurkure single pouches are commonly at this rung (grammage unverified — see OPEN QUESTIONS).

**₹30–₹40 — the "one proper pack" rung.**
- Lay's India's Magic Masala 90 g, MRP ₹35 (verified).
- Britannia Marie Gold 213.5 g, MRP ₹40 (verified).
- This is where price increases are actually taken, because the pack is no longer coin-anchored.

**₹50–₹60 — the family/share rung.**
- Britannia Good Day Cashew 250 g, MRP ₹55 (verified).
- Good Day family packs are moving to ₹120–₹140 in 2026 (Outlook Business), i.e. this rung is being vacated upward.

**₹80–₹110 — where better-for-you starts, and where mainstream ends.**
- RiteBite Max Protein single bar, ₹80 (verified). **This is the first rung at which a protein bar exists at all.**
- Haldiram's Aloo Bhujia 400 g family pack, ₹110 (verified).
- Note what this means: *the cheapest single protein bar costs about the same as a 400 g family tin of namkeen.*

**₹100+ — premium indulgence and the protein-bar mainstream.**
- The Whole Truth bars run ₹104–₹181 per bar.
- Yogabar Power Up 70 g, ₹125.
- Sunfeast Dark Fantasy 230 g, MRP ₹205; Cadbury Dairy Milk Silk 140.5 g, MRP ₹399.
- Crucially, a single better-for-you bar sits at the same rung as an entire premium biscuit pack or a large Silk bar meant for sharing.

## Magic price points and what management says about them

**The mechanism, stated by an analyst and unchallenged by management.** Abneesh Roy (Nuvama), on Britannia's Q3 FY25 earnings call, 7 February 2025:

> "My first question is on the current very high inflation. So my specific question is in the popular price point of, say, INR5 and INR10, till now how much grammage cut you would have taken because there you can't increase prices; and on the larger packs, how much pricing you've already taken?"

Varun Berry (Vice Chairman & MD) answered with the aggregate, not a denial: total price increase ~₹100 crore in Q3, "about 2%, but it's progressively going up. By the end of the year, we will be at about 4%, 4.5% price increase."

**Why the coin points can't be repriced — the analyst's own framing.** Percy Panthaki, same call:

> "in biscuits, since a lot of the pricing happens through grammage changes, the price increase doesn't fully translate to a higher top line."

This is the single cleanest statement of why Indian FMCG price architecture works the way it does: at coin points, "pricing" *is* grammage, and it shows up in the P&L differently from a real price rise.

**How big the coin-point business is.** Varun Berry, quoted in Outlook Business: Britannia's "LUP of Rs 5 and 10 are approximately 50 to 55 per cent of its total mix." In the same piece, Abneesh Roy: "Most FMCG categories have lower unit packs of Re 1 to Rs 10 accounting for 25 to 35 per cent of their sales"; and Mohit Malhotra (CEO, Dabur India): "we have seen grammage reduction to protect **sacred price points** like Re 1, Rs 5, and Rs 10." HUL is described in the same article as running a "bridge-pack strategy," with ~30% of business in price-point packs.

**Bridge packs, in management's own words.** Varun Berry, Q3 FY25 call, on reaching down to an unoccupied coin point:

> "we talked about drinks where we are now coming up with a INR20 SKU, which is a very large market and our participation was only in the INR35 or INR40."

**The 2026 policy, plainly stated.** Reported by Outlook Business, 26 May 2026: Britannia would take "selective price hikes through a mix of grammage reduction in smaller packs and price increases in packs priced above ₹10," with "Smaller packs priced at ₹5 and ₹10 ... likely to avoid direct price increases, with companies instead expected to reduce grammage in those products."

**Chocolate's coin point is stepping up.** Mondelez India launched Cadbury Dairy Milk Milkinis in Sept 2025 at ₹20/17 g and ₹40/34 g. Nitin Saini, VP–Marketing, Mondelez India: "This unique milk crème format is designed to delight with a playful, shareable treat that speaks to today's evolving snacking habits."

## Shrinkflation

**The canonical documented example — Parle-G at ₹5.** Mayank Shah, Senior Category Head, Parle Products, to Business Today (22 Aug 2019):

> "We reduced volumes, so we did an indirect price hike. We have cut down the grammage of a Rs-5 pack from 70 grams to 65 grams since December 2018."

And on how consumers read it — the reason shrinkflation has a behavioural ceiling:

> "Consumers are saying that the pack size has become smaller, so, they would rather buy something else. They tend to buy something that is loose, not coming from the organised sector."

That second quote is the important one for persona modelling: **grammage cuts at ₹5 push buyers to loose/unbranded product, not to a bigger pack.**

**The 2025–26 cycle.** On the Q3 FY25 call (7 Feb 2025) Britannia described a 6%–6.5% total price increase programme, of which a large share is delivered through grammage: Berry, "6% to 6.5% price increase, 2.5% is going to be the cost efficiencies." Drivers named on the call: palm oil import duties ("now we know that it's not going away"), cocoa, and wheat. Berry also conceded the volume cost: "volumes do take a beating when there is a price increase."

**Current live example (May 2026).** Britannia's plan splits the shelf exactly along the ₹10 line — family packs repriced (Good Day Pista Badam 526 g → ₹140; Butter → ₹120; Cashew → ₹130), while ₹5 and ₹10 packs take grammage cuts instead of price rises (Outlook Business, 26 May 2026).

**Before/after grammage figures I could verify:** only the Parle-G ₹5 pack, 70 g → 65 g (Dec 2018). Specific current-cycle before/after gram figures for individual SKUs are NOT verifiable from the sources I opened — see OPEN QUESTIONS. Do not let a persona quote a specific 2026 grammage cut figure.

### The multiple

Against Cadbury Dairy Milk at **₹0.71/g**:

- RiteBite Max Protein Roots — **2.5× per gram**
- Yogabar Power Up — **2.5× per gram**
- The Whole Truth Double Cocoa — **2.8× per gram**
- The Whole Truth Pro — up to **3.8× per gram**
- Yogabar's cheapest wafer format — **1.6× per gram** (the closest anyone gets to parity)

**But per gram understates the felt cost, because the packs are not the same size.** What the buyer actually hands over at the counter:

| | Cash per single unit | Multiple vs ₹17 Dairy Milk |
|---|---|---|
| Cadbury Dairy Milk 24 g | ₹17 | 1.0× |
| Yogabar 10 g wafer bar (in a 10-pack) | ₹39.9 | 2.3× |
| RiteBite Max Protein Roots 45 g | ₹80 | **4.7×** |
| The Whole Truth Double Cocoa 52 g | ₹104.5 | **6.1×** |
| Yogabar Power Up 70 g | ₹125 | **7.4×** |
| The Whole Truth Pro 67 g | ₹157–181 | 9.2–10.6× |

**And the better-for-you brands mostly don't sell single units at all.** Whole Truth and Yogabar sell in boxes of 5–12. The real minimum ticket to enter the category is not ₹80 — it is **₹399 (Yogabar 10-pack) to ₹836–₹950 (Whole Truth 8-pack)**. Against a ₹10 or ₹17 impulse purchase, that is a two-to-three-order-of-magnitude change in the size of the decision.

**How to use this in persona reasoning:**
- "Healthy alternative to a chocolate bar" = **~2.5× the money per gram, ~5× the money per unit, and often a ₹400–₹950 minimum basket** instead of a ₹10–₹20 coin.
- A protein bar is not competing with a Dairy Milk on the impulse shelf. At ₹80–₹125 it is competing with a 400 g family tin of Haldiram's Aloo Bhujia (₹110) or a 230 g pack of Dark Fantasy (₹205) — i.e. with a whole household's snack, not one person's treat.
- The bar category is a *planned, boxed, subscription-shaped* purchase. The chocolate bar is an *unplanned, single, coin-shaped* one. They are different buying occasions with different mental accounts, and price is the reason.

## Channel price differences

- **Modern e-commerce (Flipkart, Amazon) does not stock the impulse shelf.** Flipkart's Cadbury brand page returned 38 SKUs of which essentially none was a single impulse bar — the smallest units were things like Dairy Milk Bites 40.1 g (₹190 MRP) and multipacks like 5 × 51 g (₹399 MRP). A search for "Cadbury Dairy Milk 13.2 g" returned gift baskets and rakhi combos, not the ₹10 bar. Searches for Kurkure and Bingo returned almost no first-party SKUs at all.
- **Consequence: e-commerce MRPs are multipack MRPs.** A "Dairy Milk" on Flipkart is ₹399 for 5 × 51 g; a "Dairy Milk" in a kirana is ₹10–₹20. Any persona reasoning about "what a Dairy Milk costs" must use the kirana/q-commerce number, never the Flipkart one.
- **Pharmacy/health channel (Tata 1mg) carries genuine single units** and was the only channel I could open that listed a real single Dairy Milk bar (24 g, MRP ₹17) — a useful and underrated source for single-serve MRP.
- **Brand D2C carries only boxes.** Neither Whole Truth nor Yogabar's own store leads with a single bar; RiteBite is the exception, selling singles at ₹80.
- **Quick commerce (Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart, BigBasket) could not be opened** — all returned 403 or JS-only shells. Their listings are visible in search-engine titles and do appear to carry the true single-serve packs at distinct prices, but I have deliberately excluded every one of those numbers. See OPEN QUESTIONS.

## Open questions

These are unresolved *because I would not print a price I could not open*. Each is a genuine gap, not an oversight.

1. **The Cadbury Dairy Milk small-unit ladder is not verified.** The 13.2 g @ ₹10 and 23 g @ ₹20 rungs are widely cited and almost certainly real, but every listing carrying them (BigBasket, Blinkit, Zepto, JioMart, Amazon.in) was unopenable. **The only Dairy Milk single unit I verified is 24 g @ ₹17 MRP (1mg).** Note that a BigBasket search-result title rendered this SKU as "132 Gm ... at Best Price of Rs 10" — a dropped decimal on 13.2 g, and a good illustration of why snippet-scraped prices are dangerous.
2. **Cadbury Silk, Crackle, Fruit & Nut, Munch, Perk and 5 Star single-bar MRPs** — not verified. Only multipack/home-treats formats were openable.
3. **Ice cream is entirely missing.** Amul's own store (shop.amul.com, amul.com) returned 404/403; Kwality Wall's single-serve was not openable. **No Amul or Kwality Wall's price in this lane is verified — do not let a persona quote one.**
4. **Kurkure and Bingo have no verified price.** The one Flipkart Kurkure page that opened rendered the price as "₹1" for a 75 g pack, which is plainly a parse/display error and has been discarded rather than reported.
5. **Haldiram's small (₹10/₹20) namkeen pack** — not verified; only the 400 g family pack at ₹110.
6. **Biscuit rungs not verified:** Britannia 50-50, Bourbon, Milk Bikis single-pack MRPs.
7. **The Whole Truth undiscounted list prices** — the storefront showed post-promo-code prices. The MRP-equivalent is unknown, so the trade-up multiple above may be *understated*.
8. **Current-cycle before/after grammage figures** for specific 2025–26 SKUs could not be sourced. Only the 2018 Parle-G 70→65 g cut is documented with numbers.
9. **Street comparators are only partly sourced.** Pav price (~₹3→₹4, FPJ, May 2026) is solid; specific 2026 metro prices for samosa, vada pav and cutting chai are not verified from a named outlet, only from travel/aggregator blogs, and have been excluded.

## Handling notes for the persona pack

- **Safe to put in a persona's mouth:** Parle-G ₹10 for 90 g; a Dairy Milk around ₹17–₹20; Lay's ₹35 for 90 g; Good Day ₹55 for 250 g; a protein bar at ₹80–₹125 and a Whole Truth box at ₹800–₹950.
- **Not safe:** any Amul/Kwality Wall's price, any Kurkure/Bingo price, any specific 13.2 g/₹10 or 23 g/₹20 Dairy Milk claim stated as fact, any 2026 grammage-cut figure.
- **The behavioural rule that matters most:** at ₹5 and ₹10 the pack shrinks and the price never moves, so a buyer at those rungs experiences inflation as *less biscuit*, not as *more money* — and reacts by switching to loose/unbranded product, exactly as Parle observed.
