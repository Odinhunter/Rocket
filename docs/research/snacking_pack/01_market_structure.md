## Detail

### 1. Size and growth of Indian snacking sub-categories

**Savoury snacks / namkeen — the best-sourced sub-category by a wide margin.**

Source: Frost & Sullivan, *A Frost & Sullivan Report — February 2024*, the industry report
prepared for the Gopal Snacks Limited IPO prospectus (a named research firm publishing on the
record in a SEBI-filed document).

> "The Indian market for savoury snacks is estimated to be worth INR 796 billion in FY2023E and
> is projected to grow at a CAGR of 11% reaching INR 1,217 billion by 2027F."

> "Western snacks dominate the Indian savoury snack market and is valued at INR 409 billion
> holding a market share of 51%. The western snack industry comprises of chips, extruded snacks,
> snack pellets and other western snacks varieties including bridges and nachos among others. On
> the contrary, the market valuation for ethnic savouries (including gathiya) is estimated at INR
> 388 billion, where it contributes 49% to the overall Indian savoury snacks market. The popular
> types of traditional snack marketed in India include namkeen, bhujiya sev, snack mixtures,
> gathiya along with other ethnic savoury snack varieties such as mathari, chakali, dry samosa,
> kachori among others."

Within the **organised** savoury snacks market (INR 452 bn, FY2023E) — Exhibit 30 chart values:
Ethnic Namkeen & Snacks **179 (40%)**, Gathiya **14 (3%)**, Western Snacks **259 (57%)**, INR bn.

> "In FY 2023E, western snacks dominated the organized market and holds a major market share of
> 57% in the Indian savoury snacks segment. On the other hand, ethnic namkeen and snacks together
> contributed to ~40% of the organized savoury market."

> "In FY 2023E, the organized western snacks market in India is valued at with INR 259 billion
> and is projected to register a CAGR of 12.1% till FY 2027F. Chips contributes towards the
> largest market share of ~53% followed by extruded snacks and puffs (~22%) in FY 2023E."

> "In FY 2023E, the organized Indian ethnic namkeen and snacks market is valued at INR 179
> Billion." (FY2018: 137; FY2027F: 272; CAGR 2023E-27F: 11.0%)

> "In FY 2023E, the organized market for Gathiya stood at INR 14 billion accounting for 3% of the
> total organized Indian savoury snack market and it is forecasted to be growing at a CAGR of
> 11.7% during FY 2023E-2027F."

> "In FY 2023E, the Indian papad market is valued at INR 83 billion and is projected to reach INR
> 106 billion by 2027F with a CAGR of 6.3%."

**Regional split — matters for urban persona geography.**

> "Northern region accounts for 41% i.e., INR 159 billion in Indian ethnic savouries market…
> The northern region has been home to popular brands such as Haldiram's, Bikaji and Bikanervala.
> When it comes to western snacks, northern states account for ~34% i.e., INR 139 billion.
> Prataap Snacks is prominent in the region with their "Yellow Diamond" brand… Papad market
> accounted for INR 31 billion in northern region."

> "Western region is the fastest growing zone for consumption of savouries and papad. The region
> accounts for 34% i.e. INR 134 billion in the Indian ethnic savouries market. It is home to
> brands such as Gopal Snacks, Prataap Snacks, Chheda Specialties, Chitale Bandhu, Haldiram Foods
> International (Nagpur) and so on."

> "Ethnic savouries and western snack together accounted for ~INR 112 billion and INR 128 billion
> for East and South region respectively. These regions tend to have higher western snacks
> consumption owing to pricing, flavours, consistent availability, and convenience of buying.
> Eastern and Southern states of India are dominated by local/regional players like Bisk Farm and
> Adyar Ananda Bhavan among other small-medium enterprises."

**Packaged food, for context:**

> "The packaged food industry in India is estimated to reach INR 4,622 billion in FY 2023E and
> projects a growth potential of ~10% during FY 2023E- 2027F."

> "Per capita spend of packaged food in 2018 was INR 2,095 and grew to INR 3,657 in 2023E. The
> per capita spend is anticipated to witness a CAGR of 11% during the forecast period of
> 2023E-2027F."

**Global framing (same report):**

> "Global sales of savoury snacks are valued at USD 113 billion in 2023E, with Asia Pacific
> leading the market and is valued at USD 46 billion… The Indian market is expected to expand at
> a CAGR of 11% through 2027F, making it the second-largest contributor after China."

### 2. Organised vs unorganised split

**Savoury snacks — organised is already the majority and gaining slowly.**

> "In FY 2023E, the organized market for savoury snacks dominated the Indian market accounting
> for a market share of ~57% with a valuation of INR 452 billion. The growth of organized sector
> in India is highly supported by the economic growth that has increased the sales via modern
> retail including supermarket and hypermarkets. The major contribution comes from leading
> established players in snack industry such as PepsiCo, which owns Lays and Kurkure, Haldiram's,
> ITC's Bingo chips and Balaji Wafers among others."

> "The organized sector contributed to INR 335 billion in FY 2018 and reached a market value of
> INR 452 billion in FY 2023E. Organized sector is anticipated to further register a CAGR of
> 11.7% during 2023E-2027F."

> "In the Indian snack industry, the organized market holds a market share of ~57% in FY 2023E
> and is forecasted to dominate the Indian market accounting for a market share of ~58% by 2027F."

Chart series (Exhibit 29), organised share: **55% (FY2018) → 57% (FY2023E) → 58% (FY2027F)**.
This is a *slow* shift, not a landslide.

**Why unorganised persists — directly usable for persona reasoning:**

> "However, despite the fact that organized players maintain a firm presence throughout India and
> most of the market share in certain regional pockets, 43% of the packaged savoury snack market
> is still unorganized in FY 2023E. The food, culture, and traditions of India vary and hence
> India's snack preferences vary by region as well. The unorganized sector caters to the local and
> regional demands. For instance, in states like Gujarat and Maharashtra, traditional snack like
> Fafda, Khakhra, Dhokla, Shankarpali and Bhelpuri are more prominent and popular. In the central
> and northern India, Bhujia Sev is consumed more as snack option. While, in southern India snacks
> prepared with fruits such as banana chips are highly popular."

> "In the snacks industry, snacks sold in local kirana shops and convenience stores continue to
> compete with well-known Indian brands even today."

**Packaged food as a whole — the reverse picture.** The savoury-snacks story is *not*
generalisable to Indian food.

> "The Indian packaged food industry is dominated by the unorganized players. The unorganized
> sector dominates the industry accounting for a market share of ~81% in FY 2023E. It is
> estimated that the unorganized sector would continue to occupy a significant market share of
> ~79% in FY 2027F"

Chart (Exhibit 18), organised / unorganised: **13/87 (2018), 18/82 (2022), 19/81 (2023E),
21/79 (2027F)**.

**Papad — still overwhelmingly unorganised:**

> "The organized papad market in India accounts for a minor share of 34% of the total papad
> market in FY 2023E."

**Sweets:** no sourced organised/unorganised split for Indian *mithai* was obtained. Open question.

### 6. Recent trends — volume vs value, urban vs rural, premiumisation, small packs, and what
management is actually saying

**GST 2.0 is the single biggest structural event in this window.** 56th GST Council meeting,
3 September 2025, Ministry of Finance press release:

> "Rationalisation of the current 4-tiered tax rate structure into a citizen-friendly 'Simple
> Tax' - a 2 rate structure with a Standard Rate of 18% and a Merit Rate of 5%; a special
> de-merit rate of 40% for a select few goods and services"

> "Reduction of GST from 12% OR 18% to 5% on almost all of the food items such as packaged
> namkeens, Bhujia, Sauces, Pasta, Instant Noodles, Chocolates, Coffee, Preserved Meat,
> Cornflakes, Butter, Ghee, etc."

> "Reduction of GST from 5% to NIL on Ultra-High Temperature (UHT) milk, Prepackaged and labelled
> chena or paneer; All the Indian Breads will see NIL rates (Chapati or roti, paratha, parotta,
> etc)"

The reduced rates were effective **22 September 2025**. Annexure-I ("GOODS", HSN-wise rate
changes) explicitly lists the snacking headings: **1704 Sugar confectionery**, **1806 Chocolates
and other food preparations containing cocoa**, **1905 Pastry, cakes, biscuits and other bakers'
wares**, **1905 90 30 Extruded or expanded products, savoury or salted**, **2105 00 00 Ice cream
and other edible ice**, and **2106 90 Namkeens, bhujia, mixture, chabena and similar edible
preparations ready for consumption form (other than roasted gram), pre-packaged and labelled**.

⚠ The per-line "from X% to Y%" columns of Annexure-I did not survive PDF text extraction. Quote
the headline bullet (12%/18% → 5%) and the HSN list; **do not assert a specific old→new rate for
an individual line item** without re-opening the annexure.

**Two downstream effects are visible in company filings:**

1. It broke the market-share measurement. Britannia Q3 FY26 deck: *"In view of transition to GST
   2.0 & dual pricing on packs permitted by the Govt. in Q3, Nielsen's data needs further

> "During the year, urban markets witnessed heightened competitive intensity from regional/local

Two things worth noting: **local and regional players are taking share in urban markets**, and
**the channel is shifting under the incumbents' feet**. Both are named by ITC itself.

Actual outcome, Britannia Q3 FY26 analyst deck (Feb 2026):

- YTD Dec'25 revenue **Rs 14,172 crore**, 12-month growth **+7.7%**; PAT 13.1% of revenue.
- Annual revenue growth on the trend slide: **FY22-23 (15%), FY23-24 (4%), FY24-25 (6%), YTD

- Quarterly consolidated sales (Rs crore): 3,654 / 4,338 / 4,101 / 3,892 · 3,970 / 4,370 / 4,192
  / 4,014 · 4,130 / 4,566 / 4,463 / 4,376 · 4,535 / 4,752 / 4,885.

So: **the double-digit ambition was not met; FY26 came in around 8%** — a genuine improvement
on FY24's 4% and FY25's 6%, but not a boom.

**Commodities, Q3 FY26 vs Q3 FY25** (Britannia Q3 FY26 deck, "average actual consumption rates

**Pack architecture — family vs impulse.** Bikaji Q1 FY26 SKU mix: **Family packs 58.7% /
Impulse packs 41.1%** of sales (Q1 FY25: 57.8% / 40.6%). Family packs INR 3,583 mn (+15.8%),

leading namkeen player.

### 7. Who is winning, who is losing, and better-for-you entrants

**Winning, on the evidence opened:**

- **Nestlé India in chocolate.** *"India is now the largest market for KitKat globally, and the
  brand has accelerated its market share growth over the last few years"* — Manish Tiwary,
  Chairman & MD, Nestlé India (Outlook Business, 17 May 2026, **PRESS**). The same article
  reports India ranked **number 10 globally for KitKat a decade ago**, and quotes Jagatheesan
  Gopichandar, Director of Confectionery and Cereals, attributing growth to *"strong increase in
  core penetration and also entry into new consumer demand spaces."* It further cites Nestlé
  India's FY25 annual report as stating the company produced *"3,950 million fingers of KitKat
  after doubling its market share"* — **this last figure is second-hand and should be re-verified
  against the Nestlé India annual report before use.**
- **Bikaji.** +14.2% value / +7.5% volume in Q1 FY26 against a subdued market, direct reach up
  4.5x in three years, margin expanding on softer inputs, buying into premium retail (The
  Hazelnut Factory) and going cross-border (C.G. Foods Nepal JV).
- **Britannia's adjacencies.** Cake, rusk, croissant and wafers *"All 4 categories grew
  double-digit during the quarter"* while the core biscuit business grew mid-single-digit.

**Losing, or under pressure:**

- **Kwality Wall's / the ice-cream incumbent.** Q3 FY26 organic sales **-6.5%**, EBITDA loss
  **₹64.2 crore**, exceptional items ₹94 crore, in-home portfolio *"saw a muted response and is
  planned to be relaunched with an improved offering for the 2026 season."* A newly-independent
  national ice-cream leader is currently shrinking and loss-making.
- **ITC's FMCG margin.** Segment EBITDA fell to **INR 2,163.92 cr from INR 2,338.50 cr** even as
  revenue rose — margin compression from input costs and *"significant increase in competitive
  intensity from local/regional players."*
- **The incumbents' urban share generally.** ITC names *"heightened competitive intensity from
  regional/local players"* in urban markets specifically.

**Better-for-you and high-protein — what is verified:**

- **Yogabar / Sproutlife Foods** is the clearest verified entrant, and it is now effectively
  inside ITC. ITC Report and Accounts 2025: *"Sproutlife Foods Private Limited (Sproutlife)
  operates in the fast growing, nutrition-led health food space with a diversified product
  portfolio across multiple categories including protein bars, breakfast cereals, nutraceuticals
  etc. under the 'Yogabar' brand."* And: *"Sproutlife continues to register robust growth across
  its target markets in its core categories of Bars, Muesli and Oats; product portfolio was
  augmented during the year with the launch of protein drinks and whey protein."* ITC stake
  **47.50%** fully diluted at 31 Mar 2025, cumulative investment **INR 255 crore**.
- **ITC is also buying its way into adjacent better-for-you space.** Report and Accounts 2025:
  *"several value accretive acquisitions were announced during the year viz. M/s. Sresta Natural
  Bioproducts (24 Mantra Organic Foods), Mother Sparsh Baby Care (Mother Sparsh) and Ample Foods
  (Prasuma & Meatigo)."*
- **Mainstream brands are reformulating rather than launching new better-for-you brands.** ITC on
  Bingo!: *"Bingo! forayed into the Popped Chips segment with the launch of three exciting
  variants - 'Sour Cream & Herbs', 'Salt n Pepper' and 'Indian Spice Mix', with 30% less fat
  proposition for consumers indulging in mindful snacking"*, plus a *"Millet based offering under
  'Bingo! Tedhe Medhe Pudina Twist'"*. ITC also reports *"Over 100 new products anchored on the
  vectors of Health & Nutrition, Hygiene, Protection & Care, Convenience & On-the-Go, Indulgence
  etc., were launched across target markets during the year."*
- Frost & Sullivan (FY2023E vintage) on the demand shift: *"rising demand for 'better-for-you'
  ingredients is anticipated to support the growth of healthier and premium products such as
  baked snacks in the Indian market"* and *"The demand for low-calorie and low-sugar snacks has
  increased both internationally and in India."*

**Not verified:** The Whole Truth, MuscleBlaze / HealthKart, RiteBite Max Protein, Open Secret,
Farmley and the rest of the D2C protein-snacking cohort. No page for these was opened. See Open
questions. One indirect signal that D2C is on incumbents' minds — an analyst put it to Britannia
directly on the Q4 FY25 call: *"a lot of FMCG categories, there is a competition from D2C… In
your categories, is there an impact, especially now I'm seeing Tata's Soulfull acquisition now
going to Rusk"* — and Berry's answer did not quantify it.

### E. Method notes for whoever continues this
- `nestle.in`, `hul.co.in`, `kwalitywallsindia.com`, `pib.gov.in`, `business-standard.com`,
  `aninews.in` and `pwc.in` all returned **HTTP 403** to automated fetching. Company PDFs on
  `media.britannia.co.in`, `bikaji.com`, `itcportal.com`, `gstcouncil.gov.in` and
  `nsearchives.nseindia.com` fetched cleanly with a browser user-agent.
- `bseindia.com/xml-data/corpfiling/AttachLive/*.pdf` returns an HTML interstitial, not the PDF,
  even with a Referer header. **NSE's `nsearchives.nseindia.com` is the working route to filed
  documents** — that is how the Kwality Wall's release was obtained.
- PDF text extraction (pypdf) loses table *columns*. Chart data labels and rate-change columns
  frequently survive as unlabelled number runs. **Never infer which column a number belonged
  to** — that is exactly how a fabricated statistic gets born.
