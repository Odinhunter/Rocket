## ⭐⭐ THE HEADLINE: THE SUBSTITUTION IS REAL AND IT IS ECONOMIC, NOT PREFERENTIAL

The pre-registration named **three independent routes** by which this cell could produce
beverage-for-snack substitution. Exactly one of them fired, and it is not the one the design doc
is about.

| route, as pre-registered | fired? |
|---|---|
| 1. **pantry / vending, either-or** — the desk worker who takes a coffee instead of eating | ❌ **zero** |
| 2. **canteen / thela, either-or on price** — ₹15 buys the chai or the vada, not both | ✅ **both instances** |
| 3. **the weight-conscious skip** — *"black coffee now, stopped the biscuit"* | ❌ **zero** |

⭐⭐ **And the actual desk workers in the sample all kept their snacks.** The procurement manager
on ₹22L, the Mumbai CA, the Bengaluru bank analyst, the draughtsman — every one of them is silent
about beverages and organised around a bar, a biscuit or a box. **The men who substituted are a
lift fitter, a data-entry clerk, a diamond polisher, a leather-press operator and a grain-market
loader, on ₹2.2L–₹5.5L.**

⭐ **So the design doc's sentence survives, RE-SCOPED, and this is the finding worth the $0.57:**

> *"chai wins the 4pm dip in India more often than any biscuit"*

**is true when the chai is free or ₹10 and the snack is priced, for a man on ₹3-5L.** It is a
**price** substitution among cash-constrained workers — not the metro office worker's
**preference** the doc describes. The doc imagined the wrong buyer for its own claim. ⭐ That is
more useful than a clean pass: it says where in the map beverage-vs-snack competition actually
lives, and it is not where the doc put it.

## ⚠⚠ THE HONESTY NOTES — read these before quoting the verdict anywhere

1. ⚠ **At n=8, two-versus-zero is not statistically separable.** Part A's control was 0 of 8;
   this control is 2 of 8. **Fisher exact, two-tailed: p ≈ 0.47.** The ≥2 threshold was a
   **pre-committed decision rule, not a significance test**, and it is being applied as committed
   — which is the entire point of pre-registering. ⭐ **Do not report this as "significantly more
   substitution than tier-3."** Report it as: the behaviour **exists** here and did not exist
   there, on a rule fixed in advance.
2. ⚠ **It is a bare pass.** One threshold notch tighter and this reads WEAK. Say "bare" every
   time.
3. ⚠ **The population delivered was broader than the population ordered** — see the tier finding
   below. This was not a pure metro sample.
4. ⚠ **One moment, one region, one gender, one pack pair.** Nothing here speaks to the other
   seven moments, to women, to tier-2/3, to persona quality, to the tail, or to sizing.

### ⚠⚠ `--tier tier-1` IS A LABEL WITH NO LIST BEHIND IT AND NO GATE UNDER IT

**Measured: 15 of 25 people in the control and 10 of 25 in the beverage arm are in a true tier-1
metro.** The rest are **Kanpur, Patna, Lucknow, Nagpur, Indore, Coimbatore, Surat, Jaipur, Kochi**
— tier-2 by any standard Indian classification, every one of them labelled `/ tier-1`.

**Both halves of the defect are in the code:**
1. **The spec is a bare label.** The schema's `geography` description says *"Use the tier the
   region declares, spelled exactly 'tier-1'/'tier-2'/'tier-3'… Real places only, and sized to the
   tier."* ⭐ **It never says what tier-1 means in city terms**, so the model honoured the label
   25 times out of 25 and chose the cities freely. ⚠ **Same defect class as `occupation_hint`
   described as "The job"** — a field whose description is its whole spec, and a wrong one is
   invisible.
2. **No gate reads it.** `gate_people` checks **gender, age and income** against the region and
   **never looks at `geography` at all**, which is why both files passed all five gates carrying a
   Patna man in a tier-1 region.

⚠ **Identical in both arms, so it cannot touch the comparison.** But it is material to the claim
*"this tested metro men"* — ⭐ and it **folds into the headline rather than undermining it**: the
sample was broader than ordered, the substitution came from its lower-income half, and the true
metro desk workers produced none. **A purer metro sample would likely have scored LOWER, not
higher.**

## What this gives decision 3 — both sides, deliberately not picked

**If the map REPLACES the grid:** beverage-vs-snack competition is real but lives in the
**moment's own alternatives layer**, not in the category pack — proved twice. So the map must
carry branded, priced beverages inside each moment's competitive set, and the moments where it
matters are the **cash-constrained, out-of-home ones** (canteen, thela, street) rather than the
office-pantry ones the doc emphasised.

**If the map does NOT land:** the same edit is available far cheaper as the grid fix
`docs/fnb_test_a_result.md` already specified — put branded, priced beverages into
`everyday_alternatives` and into `desk_slump_4pm.competes_with`, then re-run both arms for
~$0.57. **This run tightens that fix**: price the beverage against the snack, because price is
the mechanism that fired.

⚠ **Either way the pack is not where beverages go.** That question is now closed at two cells.
