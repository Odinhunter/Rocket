# Brand-Manager Sessions — the commercial gate (+ backtest)

**What this tests — and what it does NOT.** A brand manager saying "I'd pay for
this" tells you the deliverable is **compelling**, not that it's **true**. Reading a
confident report, they can't tell a real diagnosis from a fluent, plausible-but-wrong
one. So this track answers two questions:

1. **Would they buy it?** (desirability / product-market-fit — the fastest way to kill
   or greenlight the whole thing, with people you can reach now.)
2. **Does the engine's verdict match what the ad actually did in-market?** (the
   backtest — *only if they bring their own ads with known outcomes*.)

It does **NOT** certify the diagnosis. What you sell is the *why + the fixes*, and an
ad can underperform for reasons other than the pains the engine named. Only the
**consumer panel** (pain-overlap) certifies the mechanism. Keep the two boxes
separate: brand managers = "will they buy"; consumers = "is it true."

---

## The one variable that makes or breaks a session: whose ad?

- **Their OWN ad, outcome known** (it flopped / crushed / they have CTR·ROAS) → one
  session gives you desirability **and** a backtest point **and** borrowed ground
  truth. **Build every session around this.**
- A random ad they don't know the outcome of → you only learn the format is
  appealing. Fine, but it validates nothing.

Ask each contact ahead of time to bring **1–3 ads they ran and know the result of** —
and to *not tell you the outcome* until the session.

---

## The session flow — predict, then reveal (≈20–30 min)

Designed so a warm contact can't just nod along.

**1. Pick the ad.** They choose one of their known-outcome ads. Don't reveal the
outcome yet. (You'll need the creative to run it through the engine beforehand — see
"prep" below.)

**2. Predict — BEFORE showing the report.** Capture, in their words:
- What did this ad actually do in-market? (their real outcome — now they say it)
- What did they think the top 2–3 problems were? What's working?

This does triple duty: it's borrowed ground truth, it's their honest read to compare
against, and it stops them reacting to the report with pure hindsight agreement.

**3. Reveal the engine's report** for that ad (the Creative Read deliverable).

**4. Capture their reaction** on four things:
- **Verdict match** — did the engine's call (scale / iterate / retarget / rebuild)
  match what the ad actually did? *(backtest signal)*
- **Diagnosis vs their theory** — do the engine's pains match why *they* think it
  under/over-performed? Where do they differ, and does either side have data?
  *(note: their theory ≠ consumer truth — it's a second expert opinion, not ground
  truth on the mechanism)*
- **Anything new?** — is there a single thing here they didn't already know, or would
  actually act on? (If it only confirms what they knew, it's not worth paying for.)
- **The behavioral question** — *not* "would you pay for this," but: **"would you have
  run this ad differently if you'd seen this first?"**

**5. The real willingness signal.** Stated price from a warm contact is near-worthless.
The signal that counts: **"would you run a live, upcoming ad through this before you
launch it?"** A yes-to-a-real-decision (or a written "pilot it") beats any number they
say out loud.

**6. Guardrail — protect the relationship and your future sale.** Tell them plainly:
*don't act on this specific report yet — it's unvalidated, and a wrong call acted on is
exactly what would burn you as a customer.*

---

## Optional but powerful: a decoy

Slip in a report for a **deliberately weak ad** (or a known-good one) without flagging
it. If they rave about the decoy as much as the real read, that's a warning about both
the product's discriminating power **and** how much to trust "looks great" — a plausible
report reads the same whether it's right or wrong. This is the cheapest guard against
the plausibility trap.

---

## What to capture (one row per ad)

| field | note |
|---|---|
| ad + brand | the creative |
| known in-market outcome | flopped / mid / won; CTR·ROAS if they have it |
| their predicted verdict + pains | captured BEFORE the reveal |
| engine verdict + top pains | from the report |
| verdict match? | Y / N — the backtest signal |
| diagnosis overlap with their theory | high / partial / none + where it differs |
| anything new / actionable? | Y / N + what |
| "run it differently if seen first?" | Y / N + what they'd change |
| "pilot a live ad through it?" | the real WTP signal |
| decoy caught? | did they distinguish it from the real read |

---

## Reading the results

**Green (greenlight the commercial thesis):**
- verdict matches on most known-outcome ads,
- diagnosis overlaps their theory often and surfaces ≥1 new/actionable thing,
- they'd change a decision / pilot a live ad, and they caught the decoy.

**Red (rethink before investing):**
- they praise everything including the decoy,
- all warmth, no specifics, nothing they'd act on,
- verdict misses on ads they know cold.

**Whatever the result:** a green here **gates further investment**, it does not gate the
consumer pain-check. You still need real consumers to certify the diagnosis you put your
name on. A pass here means "worth finishing," not "proven."

---

## Prep to run one (checklist)

- [ ] Contact brings 1–3 own ads with known outcomes (outcome withheld till session).
- [ ] Run each ad through the engine ahead of time → a Creative Read per ad
      (a paid run, ~$2/ad — needs an explicit go each time).
- [ ] Have the predict-then-reveal sheet ready (the capture table above).
- [ ] Optional: one decoy report prepared.
- [ ] Book ~30 min per contact.
