# v3 Human Panel — Recruiting Screener

**Target:** the `enthusiast_macros_lifter` — the one consumer type the MuscleBlaze
isolate ad actually speaks to. We are **not** recruiting "people who go to the gym."
We are recruiting the specific attitude: a serious, spec-literate lifter who buys
whey **powder** on a cycle and picks it on **function + numbers**, not on looks or
clean-label identity.

Everyone who passes this screener should be the human twin of one engine agent.
Everyone who *fails* is one of the five neighbor types the ad is (correctly) not for:
the clean-label aspirant, the beauty-supplement switcher, the lapsed skeptic, the
bar-only pragmatist, or the food-first purist.

---

## How to score it

A recruit is **IN** only if **all** of these are true:

1. **Frame match** — passes Section 0 (Q1–Q2).
2. **All four hard gates** — Section 1 (Q3–Q6) all "yes."
3. **Attitudinal fit** — Section 2: at least one of their top-2 drivers is a
   *target* driver (protein-per-scoop / third-party certification / price-per-serving),
   AND their single #1 driver is **not** taste, aesthetics, or "plant-based/clean."
4. **Zero disqualifiers** — Section 3: every statement answered "No."
5. **Literacy check passes** — Section 4: the open answer is specific and real
   (names an actual brand + a plausible protein-per-scoop number). This is the
   ungameable one — a real lifter answers in five seconds; a poser stalls.

Any single failure → **OUT** (route them out politely; don't tell them why).

> Keep the screener **blind to what we're testing.** Don't mention MuscleBlaze,
> "the ad," isolate, or that there's a follow-up study about a creative. It's a
> survey about protein-buying habits. Telling them the target biases who
> self-selects in.

## Section 0 — Frame match + composition record

Q1–Q2 are gates. **Q2b–Q2c are not gates — always record them.** They let us lay the
human panel's makeup next to the engine's five demographic bundles and caveat
honestly (see "The n=50 reality" note at the bottom — with a small connection-sourced
panel, *who* the 50 are matters more than how many).

**Q1. Age:** `___`  → must be **18–44**.

**Q2. Gender:** ☐ Male ☐ Female ☐ Other
→ must be **Male** *(a matching rule, not a judgement; the engine's panel for this
type is all-male, so to compare like-with-like the human panel is too. To include
female serious-lifters, say so and I'll add a matching female bundle to the engine
side.)*

**Q2b. Where do you live?** ☐ Metro / tier-1 (Bangalore, Mumbai, Delhi, …)
☐ Tier-2 city ☐ Smaller town / tier-3 — *record, don't gate.*

**Q2c. Roughly, your annual income band?** ☐ Under ₹3.5L ☐ ₹3.5–7L ☐ ₹7–17L
☐ ₹17–40L ☐ ₹40L+ — *record, don't gate.* (These are the engine's five bundles;
we want the 50 to **spread** across them, not clump in one.)

## Section 1 — Hard behavioral gates (all four must be "Yes")

**Q3. How often do you train with weights / resistance?**
☐ 4+ times a week ☐ 2–3 times a week ☐ Once a week or less ☐ I don't
→ **Pass = 2–3 or 4+.** (Casual / non-lifters are out.)

**Q4. How long have you trained seriously (a real routine, not on-and-off)?**
☐ 3+ years ☐ 1–3 years ☐ Under a year ☐ Not really a routine
→ **Pass = 1+ year.** (The target is settled into it, not a beginner.)

**Q5. Do you currently use whey / protein *powder* (a tub you scoop), and have you
bought a tub in the last ~3 months?**
☐ Yes, use powder + bought recently ☐ Use powder but not recently
☐ I use bars / ready-to-drink, not powder ☐ I don't use protein products
→ **Pass = first option only.** (This gate alone removes the bar-only pragmatist,
the lapsed skeptic, and the food-first purist.)

**Q6. Who decides which protein brand you buy?**
☐ I do, I research and choose it ☐ I buy whatever's recommended / on offer
☐ Someone else buys it for me
→ **Pass = first option.** (The target owns the decision.)

## Section 2 — What drives the choice (attitudinal fit)

**Q7. When you're choosing which protein to buy, which TWO things matter most?**
*(pick exactly two)*

- ☐ Protein per scoop / grams of protein *(target driver)*
- ☐ A third-party certification seal — Labdoor, Informed-Choice/Sport *(target driver)*
- ☐ Price per serving / protein-per-rupee *(target driver)*
- ☐ Taste and mixability *(neighbor — not disqualifying alone, but not target)*
- ☐ That it's plant-based / clean-label / natural *(neighbor: aspirant)*
- ☐ How the brand looks / whether it fits my feed *(neighbor: aspirant)*
- ☐ Skin / hair / general-wellness benefits *(neighbor: switcher)*
- ☐ A friend's or coach's recommendation *(neutral)*

→ **Pass = at least one of the two is a target driver, AND their #1 pick (ask them
to say which of the two is #1) is not taste, aesthetics, or "plant-based/clean."**

**Q8. Before you buy, do you check the protein-per-scoop number and look for an
authenticity / certification seal?**
☐ Always ☐ Usually ☐ Sometimes ☐ Never
→ **Strong signal = Always/Usually.** (Not a hard gate on its own; combine with Q7.
A target lifter does this reflexively.)

---

## Section 3 — Disqualifiers (any "Yes" → OUT)

Read each as a statement; "Yes, that's me" removes them. These isolate the five
neighbor types, so we don't dilute the target.

- **Q9.** "I mostly get my protein from whole food, and I try to avoid supplements
  on principle." → *purist_food_first*
- **Q10.** "I mainly use protein bars or ready-to-drink shakes — not powder tubs."
  → *pragmatist_protein_snacker*
- **Q11.** "The **main** reason I pick a protein is that it's plant-based,
  clean-label, or natural." → *aspirant_clean_label*
- **Q12.** "I buy supplements mostly for skin, hair, or beauty benefits (collagen,
  biotin, and the like)." → *switcher_results_chaser*
- **Q13.** "I used to use whey but stopped, and I'm pretty skeptical of the whole
  category now." → *skeptic_lapsed_protein*

---

## Section 4 — Literacy check (ungameable)

**Q14 (open text).** "What protein powder are you using right now — brand and how
much protein per scoop — and roughly what do you pay per tub?"

→ **Pass = a specific, real answer:** an actual brand (MuscleBlaze / Optimum
Nutrition / Avvatar / GNC / The Whole Truth / etc.), a plausible protein-per-scoop
(roughly 20–30 g), and a ballpark price. Vague ("some whey, not sure") or implausible
answers → OUT. A real spec-literate lifter answers this instantly.

## Notes

- **Sample target:** ~150–200 completes through **both** moments (Part 1 = the
  glance, Part 2 = the follow-up a day or two later). Expect drop-off between the
  two, so recruit/qualify with a cushion (aim to qualify ~250 to net ~180 who finish
  both).
- **Gender/age is a mirror rule, not a values call.** The engine panel for this
  disposition is male 18–44 across five income bands. Matching the human panel to it
  keeps the comparison clean. Widening it is fine — it just means adding a matching
  bundle on the engine side so we're still comparing like with like.
- **Keep it blind.** No mention of the brand, the ad, or "creative testing" anywhere
  in the recruit or the screener. It's a study about how people choose protein.
- **Incentive.** Plan a small thank-you (₹150–300 or equivalent) *per part completed*,
  and make Part 2 worth finishing (people ghost the follow-up) — this is the single
  biggest risk to *finishing* the panel.

### The n=50 reality (read before recruiting from your own network)

At ~50 people the risk that matters is **not** the small number — it's **who the 50
are.** A panel pulled from your own connections is a convenience sample: it will lean
toward your world (metro, higher-income, keener) while the engine deliberately spans
the tier-2/3 college trainee on a tight budget up to the senior metro pro. If all 50
are one slice, "the engine matches the humans" only means it matches *that slice*, and
no amount of extra people fixes a lopsided mirror. So:

- **Recruit for spread**, not just your closest gym friends — deliberately pull some
  tier-2/3 and some younger / lower-income lifters (Q2b/Q2c). This is a recruiting
  instruction, not an analysis split: keep the 50 **pooled** for the pain read.
- **Log every code's Q1/Q2b/Q2c**, so we can show the human makeup beside the engine's
  and state honestly how well they line up.

**What 50 can and can't do** (the full reasoning is in the chat): 50 is enough to
*kill* the instrument or *fail to kill* it on the two questions that matter — do the
engine's pains match real pains, and does it sound human. It is **not** enough to set a
precise pass/fail number; that calibration is a separate, later job for a larger,
recruited sample. A "pass" at 50 from your contacts is **provisional evidence, not a
final stamp.**
